Spatial Narrative  /  Student Guide

Write a story that only exists in one place

Spatial Narrative lets you write text, images and sound that appear only when someone physically walks up to a spot on the map. The whole class builds one story together, a bit at a time, in any order, on your own schedule.

What this is

Each story point (also called a node or passage) is a spot on a map with something to read, see or hear. A reader walks around with their phone. When they get close to a point and stay a moment, it opens.

A compass on the screen then points toward the next story points that the author chose. Following the compass leads the reader from point to point.

It is built on Twine, an open-source tool for non-linear stories. It was made so that stories could be tied to the land and only experienced in the place they were written for.

The assignment

This is an ongoing, class-wide exquisite corpse: a story built piece by piece, where each writer responds to what came before.

You do not need to wait for a group or a turn. Whenever you are ready, write one node. It can be fiction, poetry, prose, a sound, an image, or any mix. Write it at the place where you put it if you can, and use your surroundings as inspiration.

Pick one of two ways to start:

A. Continue someone's node

Read a classmate's node, take it as inspiration, then write a new node at a new place that follows from it. Then add their node under Linked From on yours, so theirs points to yours.

B. Start a new thread

Write your own node and tick Linked from Start so readers can begin there. Others can then link their nodes to yours.
Why linking mattersA node nobody links to cannot be found by a reader. A reader who has not opened anything yet only sees nodes that are linked from Start. After that, they only see the nodes that the node they are standing in points to. Links are how the story grows.

Step 1: Read the story outside

Do this first so you know what already exists. Use your phone, outdoors, in a browser.

  1. Open the reader.
  2. Tap Start geolocation and allow location access.
  3. Tap Enable compass and allow motion/orientation access.
  4. Follow the compass toward a story point. When you arrive, wait a few seconds. It loads once you have stayed inside its radius long enough.

Not outside right now? You can still read nodes in the authoring tool (see step 3).

Step 2: Log in to the authoring tool

  1. Open the authoring tool. It works on a phone or a laptop.
  2. In Class code, type the class code from your instructor.
  3. In Your name, type any name you want others to see.
  4. Tap Join.
Where is the class code?Your instructor gave it to you in class, and it is on your handout. It is not printed on this page on purpose, so that only our class can edit the story.

After joining you will see Reload, Save and Publish at the top, and a map with blue dots. Each blue dot is an existing story point.

Step 3: Choose where your node goes

Look at what exists

On a phone, use the buttons at the bottom to switch between Map, Passages, Editor and Raw. On a laptop the map is on the left and the panels are on the right.

Tap a blue dot, or pick a name in Passages, to open it in the Editor tab and read what the author wrote.

Pick your spot

Tap an empty place on the map. A small box asks for a name, so type one and tap Create.

Map will not let you click?Sometimes you have to tap an existing blue dot first, or tap the Passages tab, before the map accepts a new tap. Try that and tap the map again.

Step 4: Make your node

Your new node opens in the Editor tab. Scroll down through the fields.

FieldWhat it does
Radius (metres)How close a reader must be to trigger your node. New nodes start at 20 m. Use a larger number for open spaces and a smaller one for a precise spot, like a bench or doorway.
Dwell (ms)How long a reader must stay inside the radius before it opens. The number is in thousandths of a second, so 12000 means 12 seconds.
LedeA short italic subtitle under the title.
Body TextYour writing. Write plain prose here. Do not type links in this box; links are added separately in step 5.
ImageChoose a photo and tap Upload.
AudioUpload a sound file, tap Record to record one right there, or pull the sound out of a video (see below).

The fields Facing Threshold, After, Then and Directional Variants are optional extras. You can skip them for your first node. They are explained in Going further.

Get audio from a video

Filmed something on your phone and want only its sound? Under Audio, find Or get audio from a video.

  1. Choose a video file (mp4, mov, m4v or webm). Short clips work best, and the limit is 100 MB.
  2. Tap Extract audio.
  3. Wait while it works. The first time, it downloads a converter of about 30 MB, so it can take a minute on slow Wi-Fi. After that it is quicker.
  4. When it finishes, the sound is attached to your node as an MP3 and a player appears so you can check it. Then tap Apply Changes, Save and Publish.
Only the sound is keptThe video is converted on your own device and is never uploaded or shown to readers. If you want people to see the picture, use the Image field with a still photo instead. To swap the sound later, tap Remove next to the audio player and add a new one.

Step 5: Link it to the story

Links do two things: they make your node findable, and they decide where the reader's compass points after your node opens. Near the bottom of the Editor there are two lists and a checkbox.

Linked Passages vs. Linked From

SettingWhat it means
Linked PassagesNodes this node points to (outgoing). After a reader opens your node, the compass points to these.
Linked FromNodes that point to your node (incoming). This is how readers arrive at yours. It lists everything that currently links here, and you can add more.
Linked from StartA checkbox. When ticked, a reader who has not opened anything yet can begin at your node. It also shows up as a Start chip under Linked From.

To add to either list, choose a node from the drop-down under it. To remove one, tap the ✕ on its chip.

If you continued someone's node (path A)

  1. Open your node.
  2. Under Linked From, choose your classmate's node from the drop-down. Their node now points to yours.
  3. Tap Apply Changes, then Save and Publish (step 6).
Why this also saves their nodeA link is stored inside the node it starts from. So when you add your classmate's node under Linked From, the tool adds one link to their node, and Save writes that small change too. It only adds a link; their words, image and audio stay exactly as they are. Please do not edit anything else in their node.

If you want your node to lead somewhere

Under Linked Passages, choose one or more nodes the reader should be pointed toward next. Link to several and you create a branching, choose-your-own-adventure path.

If you started a new thread (path B): Linked from Start

Tick Linked from Start. Readers who have not opened any node yet will then see yours on the compass. Only tick it if your node works as a place to begin. If it continues someone else's node, use path A and leave it unticked, so new readers are not sent into the middle of the story.

Nodes are no longer linked from Start automatically, so a node without a Start tick and without any Linked From entry cannot be found by readers.

Removing a Start link

Untick Linked from Start, or tap the ✕ on the Start chip under Linked From. Then tap Apply Changes, Save and Publish. Make sure your node still has at least one other way in, or readers will lose access to it.

Step 6: Save and publish

The order matters:

  1. Tap Apply Changes at the bottom of the Editor. This keeps your edits.
  2. Tap Save at the top. Your work is now stored for the class.
  3. Tap Publish at the top. This rebuilds the public story so others can find your node.

After you publish, give it a minute or two, then reload the reader. Your node should be there.

Going further: facing, second stage, directional variants

These optional fields let one place tell more than one thing. They appear in the Editor for nodes that have a spot on the map. Use one, all, or none.

Facing Threshold

Once a reader has opened a node, the compass only shows the next nodes they are roughly facing. The default is 35 degrees either side of the direction they are pointing. Turn away and the marker fades from the list.

Type a number in Facing Threshold on the node you want to adjust. A smaller number (say 15) means the reader has to aim their phone straight at that node before it appears, which suits a hidden or hard-to-find place. A larger number (say 90) makes it easy to spot. Leave it blank to use the default.

Not applied at the startA reader who has not opened anything yet sees every Start node on the compass whichever way they face. The threshold only matters after the first node opens.

Second stage: After and Then

A second stage lets your node change if a reader stays. Think of it as the place revealing more after they have been there a while.

  1. Make the second-stage text as its own node. Tap New Passage in the toolbar, give it a name like Fountain_late, and leave Tags empty. It has no map spot of its own.
  2. Open your main node. In After (ms until second-stage), type how long to wait after it opens, in thousandths of a second. 30000 means 30 seconds.
  3. In Then (second-stage passage), choose Fountain_late from the drop-down. You can also pick New passage… there to create it on the spot.
  4. Write the second-stage text in the new node. It can have its own lede, image and audio.
  5. Tap Apply Changes, Save and Publish.

In the reader, the first text appears when the node opens. After the time you set, the page quietly switches to the second-stage text. You need both After and Then for this to work.

Directional variants

A directional variant shows different text depending on which way the reader is facing while standing in your node. Facing the river can show one passage, facing the street another.

  1. Make each variant as its own node: tap New Passage, name it something like Fountain_north, and leave Tags empty.
  2. Open your main node and tap + Add @dir Rule.
  3. Type a start and an end angle, then choose the variant from the drop-down. Compass angles run clockwise from north: 0 is north, 90 is east, 180 is south and 270 is west. So 315 to 45 would be north, but see the tip below.
  4. Add more rules for other directions. Tap ✕ on a rule to remove it.
  5. Write the text in each variant node, then Apply Changes, Save and Publish.
Tips for variantsYour main node's own text is what readers see when no rule matches, so write it to work on its own. For a range that crosses north, use two rules (for example 315 to 360 and 0 to 45) to be safe. Do not make your variants overlap. Variants do not need links of their own, because the main node carries the links.

You can combine all of these. A node can have a facing threshold, a second stage, and directional variants at once. Test it outside, because heading and timing are hard to judge from a desk.

If something goes wrong

ProblemTry this
Map ignores my tapsTap an existing blue dot, or the Passages tab, then tap the map again.
I cannot find my node in the readerDid you press Publish? Wait a couple of minutes and reload. Then check the node is reachable (next row).
Nobody can reach my nodeOpen it and look at Linked From. It needs at least one entry: a classmate's node, or Start. If it is empty, add a node there or tick Linked from Start, then Apply Changes, Save and Publish.
New readers do not see my node at the startIt is not ticked Linked from Start. Tick it only if it is a good place to begin.
I do not see a classmate's new nodeTap Reload at the top of the authoring tool.
It says someone else is editing a nodeOnly one person can edit a node at a time. This also happens when you add a link from their node to yours, since that edits their node. Wait two minutes and press Save again. If it keeps happening, tell your instructor.
I added a link by mistakeTap the ✕ on that chip in Linked Passages or Linked From, then Apply Changes, Save and Publish.
The compass does nothing on my phoneTap Enable compass and allow motion access. On iPhone it must be granted each time you open the page.
The story point will not open when I arriveStay still for the dwell time, and check that location is on and accurate. GPS can drift by 10 m or more, so authors should not set the radius too small.
I made a mistake in my nodeReopen it, fix it, then Apply Changes, Save and Publish again.
“Could not load the audio converter”The converter downloads the first time you use it. Check your connection and try again. If the school network blocks it, record the sound with Record, or upload an audio file instead.
“We could not find any sound in that video”The clip has no audio track, or it was recorded muted. Try a different clip.
“That video is too large”Videos over 100 MB are refused, and some phones run out of memory on long clips. Trim the video to a shorter clip and try again.
My second stage never appearsBoth After and Then must be filled in, and you have to stay in the node for the full After time. Remember After is in thousandths of a second.
My directional variant never showsCheck the angle range and that you chose the variant in the rule's drop-down. Then check the reader is actually facing that way. Make sure you tapped Apply Changes before Save.
The Then and Directional fields are missingThey only appear on nodes with a spot on the map. Variants and second-stage nodes do not have them, because they belong to a main node.

Words you will see

Node / story point / passageOne location with its text, image and audio. These all mean the same thing.
RadiusHow close you need to be to trigger a node.
DwellHow long you need to stay in the radius before it opens.
Linked PassagesNodes this node points to. The compass shows them after this node opens.
Linked FromNodes that point to this node. Also called incoming links.
StartThe opening of the story. Nodes linked from Start are where a new reader can begin.
Facing ThresholdHow precisely a reader must point their phone at a node, in degrees, before it shows on the compass. Default 35.
Second stage (After / Then)A second passage that replaces the first after a set time spent in the node.
Directional variantAn extra passage shown instead of the main one when the reader faces a chosen range of compass angles.
Exquisite corpseA collaborative game in which each person adds to a work after seeing only a little of what came before.
