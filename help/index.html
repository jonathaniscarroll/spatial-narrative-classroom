Spatial Narrative  /  Student Guide

Write a story that only exists in one place

Spatial Narrative lets you write text, images and sound that appear only when someone physically walks up to a spot on the map. The whole class builds one story together, a bit at a time, in any order, on your own schedule.

What this is

Each story point (also called a node or passage) is a spot on a map with something to read, see or hear. A reader walks around with their phone. When they get close to a point and stay a moment, it opens.

A compass on the screen then points toward the next story points that the author chose. Following the compass leads the reader from point to point.

It is built on Twine, an open-source tool for non-linear stories. It was made so that stories could be tied to the land and only experienced in the place they were written for.

The assignment

This is an ongoing, class-wide exquisite corpse: a story built piece by piece, where each writer responds to what came before.

You do not need to wait for a group or a turn. Whenever you are ready, write one node. It can be fiction, poetry, prose, a sound, an image, or any mix. Write it at the place where you put it if you can, and use your surroundings as inspiration.

Pick one of two ways to start:

A. Continue someone's node

Read a classmate's node, take it as inspiration, then write a new node at a new place that follows from it. Afterwards you link their node to yours.

B. Start a new thread

Write your own node with no parent. Others can then link their nodes to yours. Tell your instructor so it can be connected to the start of the story.
Why linking mattersA node nobody links to cannot be found by a reader. Readers only ever see nodes that the node they are standing in points to. Links are how the story grows.

Step 1: Read the story outside

Do this first so you know what already exists. Use your phone, outdoors, in a browser.

  1. Open the reader.
  2. Tap Start geolocation and allow location access.
  3. Tap Enable compass and allow motion/orientation access.
  4. Follow the compass toward a story point. When you arrive, wait a few seconds. It loads once you have stayed inside its radius long enough.

Not outside right now? You can still read nodes in the authoring tool (see step 3).

Step 2: Log in to the authoring tool

  1. Open the authoring tool. It works on a phone or a laptop.
  2. In Class code, type the class code from your instructor.
  3. In Your name, type any name you want others to see.
  4. Tap Join.
Where is the class code?Your instructor gave it to you in class, and it is on your handout. It is not printed on this page on purpose, so that only our class can edit the story.

After joining you will see Reload, Save and Publish at the top, and a map with blue dots. Each blue dot is an existing story point.

Step 3: Choose where your node goes

Look at what exists

On a phone, use the buttons at the bottom to switch between Map, Passages, Editor and Raw. On a laptop the map is on the left and the panels are on the right.

Tap a blue dot, or pick a name in Passages, to open it in the Editor tab and read what the author wrote.

Pick your spot

Tap an empty place on the map. A small box asks for a name, so type one and tap Create.

Map will not let you click?Sometimes you have to tap an existing blue dot first, or tap the Passages tab, before the map accepts a new tap. Try that and tap the map again.

Step 4: Make your node

Your new node opens in the Editor tab. Scroll down through the fields.

FieldWhat it does
Radius (metres)How close a reader must be to trigger your node. New nodes start at 20 m. Use a larger number for open spaces and a smaller one for a precise spot, like a bench or doorway.
Dwell (ms)How long a reader must stay inside the radius before it opens. The number is in thousandths of a second, so 12000 means 12 seconds.
LedeA short italic subtitle under the title.
Body TextYour writing. Write plain prose here. Do not type links in this box; links are added separately in step 5.
ImageChoose a photo and tap Upload.
AudioUpload a sound file, or tap Record to record one right there.

Leave the remaining advanced fields alone for now unless your instructor asks you to use them.

Step 5: Link it to the story

Links do two things: they make your node findable, and they decide where the reader's compass points after your node opens.

If you continued someone's node (path A)

  1. Finish your own node first, then tap Apply Changes, Save and Publish (step 6).
  2. Open their node from the Passages tab.
  3. In Linked Passages, add your node's name.
  4. Tap Apply Changes, then Save.

If you want your node to lead somewhere

Open your own node and, in Linked Passages, choose one or more nodes the reader should be pointed toward next. Link to several nodes and you create a branching, choose-your-own-adventure path.

If you started a new thread (path B)

Save and publish your node, then tell your instructor its name so it can be connected to the start of the story.

Only edit your own wordsYou are adding a link to someone else's node, not rewriting it. Please leave their text, image and audio exactly as they are.

Step 6: Save and publish

The order matters:

  1. Tap Apply Changes at the bottom of the Editor. This keeps your edits.
  2. Tap Save at the top. Your work is now stored for the class.
  3. Tap Publish at the top. This rebuilds the public story so others can find your node.

After you publish, give it a minute or two, then reload the reader. Your node should be there.

If something goes wrong

ProblemTry this
Map ignores my tapsTap an existing blue dot, or the Passages tab, then tap the map again.
I cannot find my node in the readerDid you press Publish? Wait a couple of minutes and reload. Then check that another node links to yours.
I do not see a classmate's new nodeTap Reload at the top of the authoring tool.
It says someone else is editing a nodeOnly one person can edit a node at a time. Wait two minutes and try again.
The compass does nothing on my phoneTap Enable compass and allow motion access. On iPhone it must be granted each time you open the page.
The story point will not open when I arriveStay still for the dwell time, and check that location is on and accurate. GPS can drift by 10 m or more, so authors should not set the radius too small.
I made a mistake in my nodeReopen it, fix it, then Apply Changes, Save and Publish again.
